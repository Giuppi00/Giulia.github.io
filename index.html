Master's Thesis – High-Performance Distributed Dynamic Mode Decomposition for Large-Scale Flow Separation Data

Overview

Institution
University of Genoa (DIME), with a period abroad at KTH Royal Institute of Technology
Supervisors
Prof. D. Lengani, Dr. M. Dellacasagrande (UniGe); Dr. A. Hanifi, Dr. M. Moniripiri (KTH)
Completion
July 2026

Objective

DNS of boundary layer separation and transition produces thousands of 3D snapshots that exceed the memory of a single node. The thesis delivers a distributed-memory parallel framework to apply Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) to such datasets and uses it to analyse a separated flat-plate boundary layer.

Methodology and Implementation

  • Python/MPI pipeline: time-partitioned parallel I/O, in-place spectral decomposition, MPI All-to-Allv redistribution so that each rank holds the full time history of a spatial subdomain
  • Windowed Exact DMD per streamwise subdomain and spectral wavenumber; distributed HDF5 storage of results; memory prediction and profiling tools
  • Validation: redistribution introduced no measurable numerical perturbation; DMD matched a reference MATLAB implementation to double-precision tolerance on a classical benchmark
  • Strong scaling study on the Leonardo supercomputer (CINECA)

Results

  • Best performance at about 20 MPI ranks; beyond that, communication overhead offsets parallel gains, consistently with Amdahl's law
  • On the DNS dataset, the dominant DMD eigenvalue modulus shows a growth–saturation–decay trend along the streamwise direction, qualitatively consistent with the Kelvin–Helmholtz mechanism and corroborated by an independent fluctuation-energy analysis

Methods and Tools

  • Programming: Python, mpi4py-style MPI, HDF5; MATLAB (reference validation)
  • Environment: HPC cluster (Leonardo, CINECA)

Turbulence Course Project – Multi-Model RANS Assessment on the NASA CRM-HL 2D Airfoil

Overview

Title
OpenFOAM Predictions against NASA TMR Reference Data
Institution
University of Genoa (DIME)
Completion
June 2026

Objective

Assess how well OpenFOAM reproduces the NASA Turbulence Modeling Resource FUN3D reference for the three-element CRM-HL airfoil (M = 0.2, Re = 5·10⁶, AoA = 16°), and identify the sources of discrepancy.

Methodology and Implementation

  • Full chain from the NASA STEP geometry to GMSH meshing (666,592 cells, wall-resolved boundary layer, y⁺ ≈ 0.16), OpenFOAM 12 compressible solver with local time stepping, and post-processing
  • Comparison of Spalart–Allmaras, a custom SA-neg built by modifying the OpenFOAM SA source, and k–ω SST on Cp, Cf, CL, Cm and Q-criterion fields

Results

  • CL = 3.638–3.671 vs 3.785 (FUN3D): 3–4% under-prediction; Cm deviates more (≈ −0.52 to −0.53 vs −0.38)
  • Good agreement on lower surfaces and flap; suction peaks under-predicted on slat and main wing. The small inter-model spread points to setup limits as the cause: reduced domain (3–9 chords instead of ~100), boundary-layer mesh covering ~26% of the estimated thickness, and gradient-limiter diffusion. CD was not compared because of blockage

Methods and Tools

  • Meshing: GMSH (OpenCASCADE)
  • Solver: OpenFOAM 12
  • Models: SA, SA-neg, k–ω SST

Wheel Aerodynamics and Turbulence Model Calibration – RANS Optimization via Dakota

Overview

Institution
University of Genoa, Department of Mechanical Engineering

Objective

Calibrate five k–ω SST closure coefficients (a₁, σk1, σk2, σω1, σω2) to improve the wake prediction of an isolated rotating tyre, using wind-tunnel total-pressure data.

Methodology and Implementation

  • Automated loop coupling the Dakota NL2SOL least-squares optimizer with a steady OpenFOAM RANS simulation (rotating wall, moving ground, 35 m/s)
  • Objective: RMS error of Cp0 against 3,780 Kiel-probe measurements on three downstream planes; numerical probes mapped to the experimental coordinates
  • Bounded search (±20% on a₁, ±15% on the others) from the baseline run (global RMS 0.2875)

Results

  • a₁ settled near 0.3136 with a marginal RMS gain; the solver stopped at iteration 29 with a false-convergence flag, attributed to numerical noise and mesh quality
  • Simulated vortex cores remain more diffused and less intense than measured: closure-coefficient tuning alone does not resolve this separated, out-of-equilibrium wake

Methods and Tools

  • Optimization: Dakota (NL2SOL)
  • CFD: OpenFOAM, k–ω SST
  • Post-processing: ParaView, Python

Bachelor's Thesis – CFD Analysis of Tip Clearance Effects in an Axial Turbine Stage

Overview

Title
CFD Analysis of Tip Clearance Variation Effects in an Axial Turbine Model
Institution
University of Genoa
Completion
July 2023

Objective

Development of a validated CFD model of the experimental axial turbine at RWTH Aachen University and investigation of the effects of rotor tip clearance on leakage losses and the associated vortical structures within the rotor passage.

Methodology and Implementation

  • Four rotor tip clearances modelled with structured block meshes and adapted wall spacing (y⁺ = 1) for accurate boundary layer resolution
  • RANS simulations with the Spalart–Allmaras turbulence model, selected for its robustness and acceptable computational cost
  • Qualitative analysis of rotor-passage flow structures through contour plots of relative total pressure, entropy and relative Mach number on axial cross-sections (z/b = 0.0 … 1.0)
  • Quantitative loss assessment via circumferentially averaged relative total pressure over the blade span and mass-flow-weighted radial averaging

Results

  • Tip leakage vortices grow in intensity with axial distance and are markedly stronger at larger clearances, driven by the higher leakage mass flow through the gap
  • Total pressure losses increase clearly with tip clearance; the affected region expands from the blade tip towards the hub

Methods and Tools

  • Mesh generation, CFD solver and post-processing: NUMECA AutoGrid5™, FINE™/Turbo, CFView™
  • Turbulence model: Spalart–Allmaras (1-equation model)

Numerical Techniques Course – CFD Analysis of the Flow around a Delta Wing

Overview

Institution
University of Genoa (DIME)
Course
Numerical Techniques, a.y. 2024/2025

Objective and Implementation

  • Steady simulation of a delta wing at 30 m/s and 15° incidence, followed by post-processing of the results
  • Unstructured polyhedral mesh (about 1.03 million elements) with local sizing on wing surfaces; the expected y⁺ was raised to fix orthogonality problems on the lateral surfaces while staying in the valid range for the model
  • Pressure-based, steady RANS with k–ω SST; no-slip walls, symmetry and static-pressure outlet

Results

  • Analysis of pressure and velocity distributions, turbulent kinetic energy, wall shear stress and surface pressure, and streamline/vortex structures

Numerical Techniques Course – CFD of a Cooled and Uncooled Gas Turbine Stator Cascade

Overview

Institution
University of Genoa (DIME)
Course
Numerical Techniques, a.y. 2024/2025

Objective and Implementation

  • Performance characterization of the cascade without cooling and with film cooling (coolant mass flow of 1.45% pressure side, 2.8% suction side, 1.1% trailing edge)
  • First-layer height estimated from a first Fluent run and a y⁺ calculator, then reduced after a too-high y⁺; grid dependency study over several meshes (the 441,999-element mesh selected for the uncooled case; 776,554 elements for the cooled case)
  • Energy equation on, k–ω SST, ideal-gas air, residual targets down to 1e-9 (1e-12 on energy)

Results

  • Fully subsonic flow, no shocks; expansion ratio about 1.56 in both cases; blade loading, temperature and boundary-layer details, heat fluxes on pressure side, suction side and trailing edge, and a comparison of pressure losses between cooled and uncooled cascades

Aerodynamics Course – S4083(A) Airfoil: XFoil versus Experimental Data

Overview

Institution
University of Genoa (DIME)
Completion
December 2024

Objective and Implementation

  • XFoil analysis at Re = 6·10⁴–3·10⁵ compared with experimental low-speed airfoil data (Selig database): lift, drag (pressure and viscous parts), polar, efficiency, pitching moment, aerodynamic centre, Cp
  • Sensitivity of the laminar–turbulent transition to Ncrit (4, 9, 12)

Results

  • XFoil over-predicts lift and efficiency and under-predicts drag, mainly at low Re and near stall, where separation and transition are poorly modelled; Ncrit = 4 gave the closest match to the measurements
